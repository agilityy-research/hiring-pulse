Which title words set each level
  • Entry level: intern, internship, junior, jr., graduate, new grad, associate, apprentice, trainee, co-op, early career, entry. "Associate" does not count for lawyers, physicians, dentists and veterinarians ("Litigation Associate", "Associate Veterinarian"), nor at a law firm, where an "Insurance Recovery Associate" is a practising lawyer.
  • Senior: senior, sr., experienced, lead (not shift, crew, store or warehouse lead), account or client director, assistant or associate vice president, an engineer, scientist, developer or analyst III ("Engineer III"). "Vice President" and "AVP" engineers, developers, analysts, scientists, traders and designers (the title grade banks and asset managers use) count as senior.
  • Staff / principal: staff, principal, distinguished, chief engineer or architect, an engineer, scientist, developer or analyst IV or higher. Not "Member of Technical Staff" (or of any other staff), "Chief of Staff", "staff accountant", "staff nurse" and similar.
  • Manager: manager, mgr, team lead, supervisor, controller. Not product, program, project, account, territory, relationship, brand, marketing, growth, content, community, office, partner(ships) or customer success managers, which are usually individual roles.
  • Director: director, dir. (not account or client director).
  • VP, head of, C-level: VP, vice president, SVP, EVP, head of, chief … officer, CEO, CFO, COO, CTO, CMO, CPO, CISO, president, general manager (not assistant, restaurant or store), founder. Not chief of staff, executive assistant, "assistant to" or executive business partner, and not a role in the "Office of the CEO".

How these figures are made

From the posting itself

The range as the posting states it. Postings rarely say whether it is base salary or, as in many sales roles, on-target earnings including commission, so the two are not separated. Equity is not counted. Where a posting lists several ranges (for example by location), the first one is used.

Three numbers per row

Median: the middle role, each counted at the midpoint of its range. Typical range: the median posted minimum to the median posted maximum. High end: one role in ten has a range top at or above this.

US roles only

The posting, or the job board's own country field, places the role in the US (a US city or state, or "Remote US"). A remote posting with no country is left out. Many states (California, Colorado, New York, Washington and others) require a range in the posting.

Counted once, checked

A title and range one company posts several times counts once. A figure needs {{ m.pay.minRoles }} roles from {{ m.pay.minCompanies }} companies, and no company may supply more than half of it.

Of {{ cov.withPayT | default: cov.withPay }} open postings that publish pay in any currency, {{ t.all.postingsT | default: t.all.postings }} are US postings with a yearly range in dollars; {{ t.all.nT }} distinct roles remain once repeats count once. {{ cov.payRejectedT | default: cov.payRejected }} ranges were left out as unreadable or implausible: for example a yearly figure below $10,000 or above $2 million, or a top more than five times the bottom (placeholders such as "$12,345–$123,456"). Employers with more than 1,000 open roles, staffing agencies, franchises and test boards are left out of every figure.

Pay is read from Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Recruitee postings. SmartRecruiters publishes pay only on each posting's own page, which we do not read yet, so its roles are not in these figures; Personio's feed carries no pay. Lever and Ashby ranges written only in the description text are not read. Methodology.
